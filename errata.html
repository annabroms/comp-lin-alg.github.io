Errata for 2026/27¶

Here we’ll list all the updates to the exercises and coursework Classroom repositories for Autumn 2026. An up to date version is in the public repository.

  • In Iterative Krylov methods for Ax=b, the last column of the Krylov matrix \(K_n\) should be \(A^{n-1}b\), rather than \(A^{n_1}b\).

  • In Iterative Krylov methods for Ax=b, the orthogonal projection of \(Av\) onto the Krylov subspace is \(PAv=\hat{Q}_n\hat{Q}_n^*Av\); the \(A\) was missing on the right-hand side.

  • In Iterative Krylov methods for Ax=b, in the exercise on recycling the QR factorisation in GMRES, the least squares matrix is \(\tilde{H}_k\) rather than \(H_k\) (and \(\tilde{H}_{k-1}\) rather than \(H_{k-1}\)). Householder transformations are reflections, not rotations.

  • In Iterative Krylov methods for Ax=b, in the GMRES convergence bound, \(p_n(A)=Vp_n(\Lambda)V^{-1}\), rather than \(Vp_n(\Lambda^s)V^{-1}\).

  • In Iterative Krylov methods for Ax=b, in “Knowing when to stop”, invertibility of \(A\) is not needed for the residual and pseudo-residual to tend to zero as \(x^k\to x^*\); it is needed for the converse, that a small residual implies a small error.

  • In Iterative Krylov methods for Ax=b, in the bound on the relative error, the denominators should be \(\|x^*\|\) rather than \(\|x\|\).

  • In Iterative Krylov methods for Ax=b, in “Knowing when to stop”, the relative error is bounded by the condition number of \(A\) (not of \(\|A\|\)) times the relative residual; if the condition number is large, a small residual can coexist with a large relative error (not a large condition number).

  • In Preconditioning Krylov methods, in the proof that a stationary method does not converge when \(\rho(C)\geq 1\), the eigenvalue condition \(|\lambda|>1\) should be \(|\lambda|\geq 1\), to include the case \(\rho(C)=1\).

  • In Preconditioning Krylov methods, in the proof that \(\rho(C)<1\) implies convergence, \(\lambda^k\) in the eigenvector expansion should be \(\lambda_i^k\).

  • In Preconditioning Krylov methods, in the proof that the eigenvalues of the symmetrically preconditioned matrix lie in \([1-c,1+c]\), the square root \(S\) should satisfy \(SS=M_s^{-1}\), rather than \(SS=M_s\).

  • In Preconditioning Krylov methods, the SOR convergence condition \(0<\omega 2\) should be \(0<\omega<2\).

  • In Preconditioning Krylov methods, in the finite difference example, \(f_k=f(k/n)/n^2\) should be \(f_k=f(k/(n+1))/(n+1)^2\), consistent with the grid spacing \(h=1/(n+1)\).

  • In Preconditioning Krylov methods, the example “Jacobi iteration for the example matrix” contained swapped spatial and eigenvector indices and used sine instead of cosine when combining exponentials. The corrected eigenvalues of \(D^{-1}A\) are \(\lambda_l=1-\cos(l\pi/(n+1))\), for \(l=1,\ldots,n\). Hence the maximum is \(1+\cos(\pi/(n+1))\), attained at \(l=n\), and the upper bound for the scaled Jacobi parameter is \(\omega<2/(1+\cos(\pi/(n+1)))\).

  • In Preconditioning Krylov methods, in the introduction to Chebyshev acceleration, the coefficients \(c_{jk}\) should be sought for \(j=0,\ldots,k\), including the coefficient of the initial guess \(x^0\). The eigenvalues to be made small are those of the matrix \(p_k(C)\), rather than of the polynomial \(p_k\) itself. After changing variables to the interval \([\alpha,\beta]\), the leading coefficient is \(2^{2k-1}/(\beta-\alpha)^k\), rather than \(2^{2k-1}/(\beta-\alpha)\). The accelerated iterate is \(y^k=\sum_{j=0}^kc_{jk}x^j\), rather than a sum with \(x^k\) in every term.

  • In Preconditioning Krylov methods, in the commented-out “Accelerated Jacobi’s method” block, the iteration matrix is \(-M^{-1}N=I-D^{-1}A\); the minus sign was missing. Also, if \(\lambda\) is an eigenvalue of \(I-D^{-1}A\), then \(1-\lambda\) is an eigenvalue of \(D^{-1}A\), rather than \(D^{-1}\).

  • In Preconditioning Krylov methods, the symmetric convergence analysis should use \(\lambda_{\max}=\|A\|_2\), rather than \(\|A\|_2^2\). The positivity argument for \(\hat{\mu}_{\min}\) uses the square of the smallest singular value of \(M^{-1}A^{1/2}\), rather than its minimum eigenvalue.

  • In Preconditioning Krylov methods, the CG preconditioner must be symmetric positive definite. The theorem placing the preconditioned eigenvalues in \([1-c,1+c]\) also assumes that \(A\) is symmetric.

  • In Preconditioning Krylov methods, the eigenvalue clustering proof uses the relation \(Cv=(1-\lambda)v\), without assuming an eigendecomposition. Eigenvalue clustering alone does not guarantee fast GMRES convergence; for diagonalizable matrices, eigenvector conditioning also enters the convergence bound. The simplified proof of stationary convergence when \(\rho(C)<1\) covers the diagonalizable case.
